Plumber in Ironville, West Hempfield Township, PA

Ironville is a village in West Hempfield Township, Lancaster County, and its homes mail under Columbia, PA 17512. This page explains where it sits, what to tell a dispatcher when you call from here, and which plumbing guides match common problems.

It is written for homeowners who search by village name, because the name on a map and the name on an envelope do not always match in this part of the township.

Where Ironville sits

Ironville is an unincorporated village in West Hempfield Township, not a borough. A historical marker places the former Ironville Fire Company site on Prospect Road just north of Fairview Road, and Ironville Pike runs through the area. Straight-line, it is roughly 1 to 2 miles southwest of the Silver Spring community, an estimate taken from map coordinates.

An old name, an unknown housing mix

An 1875 county atlas describes Ironville as a village on Chestnut Hill, laid out by John K. Smith, in an area where iron ore had been dug since about 1835. The name is old, but no source we found dates the houses, so a plumber should look at what is actually in your walls instead of guessing from the village's age.

One mailing name

Ironville has no ZIP of its own. Mail here goes under Columbia 17512, which is why the village is easy to confuse with Columbia borough. Other states also have places named Ironville, so say Lancaster County, Pennsylvania when you call.

What to tell the dispatcher when you call from Ironville

Start with your street, then the nearest main road. Prospect Road, Fairview Road and Ironville Pike are roads in this part of West Hempfield Township that help a dispatcher place an address. The ZIP printed on your mail is probably 17512, though parts of the wider Silver Spring area mail under 17575 or 17601.

Then say whether the home uses public water and sewer or a private well and septic system. That one answer changes which questions come first, so it is worth having ready before you call (717) 349-9373.

Landmarks that help place an Ironville address

These are public places with documented addresses. A dispatcher can use them to confirm that your street is in the area we cover.

  • Ironville Community Park, 711-725 Prospect Road, about 8.8 acres with a pavilion, playground and basketball court, listed by the Hempfield recCenter.
  • The former Ironville Fire Company site inside that park. The company was incorporated in 1944 and merged with the Silver Spring Fire Company in 2000.
  • Silver Spring Park, 3800 Ironville Pike, about 11 acres with ball fields and a pavilion, which marks the Silver Spring side of the same road.
  • The 17512 mailing address itself, which tells the dispatcher you are on the Columbia side of the township.

Public sewer or septic: why it changes the first questions

West Hempfield Township asks septic owners to have their pumping hauler send the pumping report to the township, which tells us septic systems are common enough in the township to be managed by notice. Whether your Ironville street has public sewer or a septic tank is something we cannot tell from here, so check your bill or ask the township.

It matters during a backup. On a septic property, a slow drain can come from the tank or drain field instead of the main line, and tank pumping is a separate job from clearing a house drain. Tell the dispatcher which setup you have, and the plumber can arrive with the right first steps. Columbia Water Company says it serves West Hempfield Township, but some properties use private wells, so ask your water utility which applies to your address.

Weather, sump pumps and frozen pipes

Nearby Landisville averages about 45 inches of precipitation and about 23 inches of snow a year. For an Ironville home, that means a sump pump in a basement pit works through wet springs and thaws, and exposed supply lines in a garage or crawlspace face winter cold.

Test the sump pump with a bucket of water before a storm season, and insulate any pipe along an outside wall. The burst pipe repair guide covers what to do if one splits.

While you wait for the plumber

These steps limit damage, and none of them needs a tool.

  • Find the main shutoff where the water line enters the house and close it if water is running freely.
  • Switch off a breaker only if the panel sits in a dry spot, and stay out of standing water.
  • Move rugs and stored boxes away from the wet area and photograph the damage for your insurer.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building first, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

How to check any plumber before you hire

Pennsylvania expects contractors doing $5,000 or more of home improvement in a year to register with the Attorney General and show the number on contracts. A written contract is required above $500. Verify any company, including us, at attorneygeneral.gov or by calling 1-888-520-6680.

Permit rules vary, so ask the West Hempfield Township zoning and permits office or its building code inspector whether your job needs one.
