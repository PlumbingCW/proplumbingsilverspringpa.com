What counts as a plumbing emergency

A plumbing emergency is any problem where water keeps causing damage or a household cannot safely use its pipes. Spraying or flooding water, sewage coming up through a floor drain, a water heater leaking onto the floor and a frozen pipe that has split all qualify. Waiting a day can ruin drywall, flooring and stored belongings.

Slower problems are different. A dripping faucet, a toilet that runs, a slow drain or a water heater that is merely loud can usually wait for a same-day appointment when available. If you cannot tell which group you are in, call and describe what you see, because the dispatcher can sort it out in a minute.

If water is spraying or flooding right now

Do these steps in order, then call the 24/7 line. Each one limits damage while the plumber is on the way.

  • Shut off the main water valve where the line enters the house, usually in the basement or a crawlspace.
  • Open one faucet on the lowest floor to drain what is left in the pipes.
  • Switch off power to any wet room if the breaker panel is in a dry spot. Never step into standing water near outlets.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area and take photos for your insurer.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building first, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not call us first.

How to check any plumber before you hire

Pennsylvania law gives homeowners tools for checking a contractor. A contractor who does $5,000 or more of home improvement work in a year must register with the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General and show that registration number on contracts, estimates, proposals and ads. You can verify a registration at attorneygeneral.gov or by calling 1-888-520-6680.

A written contract is required when work costs more than $500, and a contractor may not demand payment before the contract is signed. In most cases you can cancel within three business days of signing, with limited exceptions for emergencies. Use these rules with every company you consider, including us.
