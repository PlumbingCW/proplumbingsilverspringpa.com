Rain, snow and what they do to Landisville plumbing

Landisville averages about 45 inches of precipitation and about 23 inches of snow a year. That is enough water to test any basement drain, sump and downspout every season. The table pairs the weather with the plumbing failure it tends to cause.

A sump pump moves groundwater away from the foundation. It does not stop water that is coming from a failed pipe, which is why the first check is always the water meter.

Water in the basement after heavy rain: what to check first

  1. Check the meter

    Turn off every fixture and watch the dial. A turning dial points to a pipe leak. A still dial points to groundwater.

  2. Look at the sump

    If there is a pit, see whether the water level is rising and whether the pump is running. A pump that hums but does not move water needs service.

  3. Trace the outside path

    Look at downspouts and grading for water pooling against the foundation.

  4. Photograph and call

    Photograph the water line on the wall and call the 24/7 line if the problem is a pipe, a sewer line or a pump.

Before We Arrive: Landisville preparation

1

Shut off the main water valve

Find the valve where the water line enters the house, usually in the basement. Turn it clockwise. If your house uses a private well, shut off the pump breaker as well, because the pump will keep pushing water into a broken line.

2

Switch off power to wet areas

Wet basements often have a sump circuit, a washer and a water heater. Turn off the breakers for wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry. Leave the sump pump circuit on unless water is near its outlet.

3

Protect low spots first

Lift stored items off the floor and move them to the stairs. The lowest corner of a Landisville basement is where runoff usually collects.

4

Call the 24/7 emergency line

Name your road, your ZIP and the first sign you noticed. Say whether the sump is running and whether any water is still entering.
