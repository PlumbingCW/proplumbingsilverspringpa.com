River borough on the Susquehanna

Columbia sits on the east bank of the river across from Wrightsville, with US 30 along the north side and PA 462 through the center. When you call the emergency line, say which side of town you are on so dispatch can plan the route.

Why old river-borough lines fail the way they do

The Pennsylvania Canal operated from Columbia starting in 1833, and the Philadelphia and Columbia Railroad opened in 1834. The borough built up early, then kept building for generations after the canal closed in 1901. That means one basement can hold several eras of pipe, joined by transitions that leak first.

Wright's Ferry Mansion, built in 1738, is the oldest existing house in the borough, and plenty of newer homes have been renovated more than once. Before a wall comes down in a house like that, tracing hidden moisture with leak detection helps the opening land on the pipe instead of beside it.

Burst pipes and sewage backups in Columbia: what to do first

A spraying pipe, water coming through a ceiling or sewage rising in a floor drain is an emergency in any house. The 24/7 line takes those calls overnight, on weekends and on holidays.

  • Close the main water valve where the line enters the house.
  • Switch off power to wet rooms at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the path of the water.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer before cleanup starts.
  • A gas smell means you leave the building first, then call your gas utility or 911 from the yard or street.

Before We Arrive: protecting a Columbia home

1

Shut off the main water valve

Look where the water line enters the basement or crawlspace. Turn the valve clockwise until it stops, then open one faucet to drain the lines. If the handle will not move, do not force it. Tell dispatch.

2

Switch off power to wet areas

Standing water and outlets do not mix. If the breaker panel is in a dry spot, shut off the circuits for the wet room. If the panel is in a flooded basement, stay out and say so when you call the 24/7 line.

3

Clear and protect the area

Old borough basements often hold decades of storage. Lift valuables off the floor and slide boxes away from the leaking wall so the plumber can reach the pipe and the shutoff.

4

Call the 24/7 emergency line

Say whether the water is stopped, what you can see and which street you are on. Keep your damage photographs handy, because they help with the repair plan and with your insurer.

Signs an old Columbia drain or supply line is wearing out

  • Brown or rust-tinted water after the tap has sat unused overnight.
  • Pressure that has dropped slowly over several years at every fixture.
  • Drains that gurgle when another fixture runs, a hint of a partly blocked branch.
  • A musty smell near the basement wall closest to the street.
  • Green or white crust on exposed fittings, or pipes that sweat heavily in summer.
  • A floor drain that smells after heavy rain or after the washer empties.

Water service and sewer questions in Columbia

Columbia Water Company says it serves the borough, along with Mountville and Marietta up the river. Check your bill for the utility name, then ask where its responsibility ends and yours begins. That answer decides who pays to fix a leak between the street and the house.

If the leak turns out to be on the line from the street, Pennsylvania law requires a call to PA One Call at 811 at least three business days before any digging. The locate costs the homeowner nothing. For that kind of work, read about water service line repair before you agree to a scope.
