Winter in Mountville: why pipes freeze and split

Nearby Landisville averages about 23 inches of snow a year, and Mountville sits at an elevation near 443 feet. A pipe freezes when water inside it sits still in an unheated space for long enough. Exterior walls, garage ceilings and crawlspaces are the usual places.

The pipe usually splits where the ice sits, but the leak shows only when the ice melts. That is why a burst often appears hours after a thaw begins. If a faucet runs weak in winter, treat that as a warning and read about frozen and burst pipe repair before it becomes a flood.

What to do when a Mountville pipe freezes

Move through these steps in order. A frozen pipe that has not yet split can sometimes be thawed safely, but a split pipe cannot.

  1. Open the nearest faucet

    This lets melting water and steam escape and shows whether anything is flowing.

  2. Warm the pipe slowly

    Use a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet toward the frozen section. Never use a torch or open flame.

  3. Look for a wet spot

    Check the ceiling below, the wall behind and the floor under the pipe. A wet spot means the pipe has already split.

  4. Shut the main valve if water appears

    Close it before you call the 24/7 line so the repair starts dry.

Before We Arrive: preparing a Mountville home

1

Find and close the main valve

Look where the water line enters the foundation, often near the front wall or beside the meter. Close it with a firm clockwise turn. After winter work, make sure it opens and closes freely, because stiff valves are common after a long season unused.

2

Kill power near water

Switch off breakers only if the panel is dry. If water is falling through a light fixture or outlet, stay clear of that room and tell the 24/7 dispatcher on the call.

3

Clear the work area

Pull stored items away from the pipe and sump pit. Open crawlspace doors, open the garage door and move vehicles if the leak is in the garage ceiling.

4

Call the 24/7 emergency line

State whether the water is off, where it is coming from and the nearest cross street on Columbia Avenue or US 30. That helps dispatch plan the route into the borough.

Autumn checklist for a Mountville house

  • Disconnect outdoor hoses and close the interior shutoff to each outdoor spigot.
  • Insulate or heat-tape exposed supply lines in garages and crawlspaces.
  • Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit and watching it cycle.
  • Look at the water heater base for rust, drips or a puddle.
  • Flush a bucket of water down floor drains to confirm they carry it away.
  • Know where your main valve is and make sure everyone in the house does too.
