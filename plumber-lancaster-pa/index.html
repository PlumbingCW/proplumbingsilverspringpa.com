City, since 1818

Lancaster was founded in 1729, became a borough in 1742 and a city in 1818. A place that old has had every generation of pipe installed, repaired and replaced, often one room at a time.

Lancaster housing and the pipes inside it

Named neighborhoods such as Chestnut Hill, Cabbage Hill, the Historic East Side, Prospect Heights, Uptown, the West End, Musser Park and Mussertown tell you where a home sits. They do not tell a plumber what is in the wall. The age of the house and the history of its renovations decide that, and a single block can hold both original piping and fresh copper.

Homes built decades ago can still carry galvanized steel supply lines, cast iron drains or clay laterals. If your home has any of those, a repiping project may be cheaper to plan before a leak than after one. A plumber can look at what is visible in the basement and tell you honestly what cannot be judged without opening a wall.

What the City permit application asks of a plumber

The City of Lancaster's mechanical, electrical and plumbing permit application shows what the City expects from contractors. Use it as a checklist when you hire anyone for work that may need a permit.

  • The application has fields for a City of Lancaster licensed plumber's name and license number.
  • It asks for a Home Improvement Contractor registration number if one applies to the job.
  • It requires a certificate of insurance naming the City as certificate holder.
  • Whether your job needs a permit at all is a question for the City permit office, not for the contractor to guess.
  • Pennsylvania requires registration with the Attorney General at $5,000 or more in a year, and a written contract above $500.
  • Ask any company, including us, how it handles permits before booking.

Emergency plumbing in Lancaster: what to do first

A burst supply pipe or a sewage backup in a dense city block can reach a neighbor's wall within hours. The 24/7 line is staffed overnight and on holidays for exactly that reason.

  • Find the shutoff for your unit or the main valve for your house and close it.
  • Call the owner or property manager if you rent, and note the time you reported it.
  • Photograph every wet surface before moving anything.
  • Keep children and pets out of any room with sewage.
  • Gas odor in the building: walk out, then phone your gas utility or 911 from the sidewalk.

Before We Arrive: getting a Lancaster home ready

1

Close the main water valve

In a city rowhome the valve is usually where the line enters from the street side, often behind a basement stair or in a utility corner. A firm clockwise turn closes it. In an apartment, look for a unit shutoff before the owner's main.

2

Cut the breaker to wet areas

Switch off circuits serving the wet room only if the panel is dry. Never touch a switch or cord while standing in water. Tell the dispatcher if an outlet has been splashed or sparks.

3

Clear a path to the problem

Move furniture, laundry and stored items back from the leak and clear the route from the door to the basement. Tight city stairs and narrow doorways are easier to work through when they are empty.

4

Call the 24/7 emergency line

Give the street, the ZIP, whether you own or rent and whether the water is shut off. If a building door code or a gate applies, have that information ready.
