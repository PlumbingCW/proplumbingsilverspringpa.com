Should I Repipe My House With PEX in Silver Spring, PA?

Repiping with PEX makes sense when your supply pipes are failing in several places, not when one joint leaks. In Silver Spring, PA, the deciding facts are what your pipes are made of now, how many leaks you have had, and how much wall access the job needs.

By the Silver Spring Pro Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

The straight answer: sometimes yes, often not yet

PEX is flexible plastic tubing used for the pipes that bring pressurized water to your faucets, showers and appliances. It does not rust, it bends around corners with fewer joints, and it handles minor freeze expansion better than rigid metal. It is not freezeproof, and it should not sit in direct sunlight.

A whole-house repipe is worth serious thought if you have galvanized steel that is clogging with scale, copper with repeated pinhole leaks, or several leak repairs in a short span. One failed fitting on an otherwise sound system is usually a repair, not a repipe. A repiping contractor should be willing to tell you that.

Signs your supply pipes may be near the end

None of these proves a repipe is needed, but a cluster of them is worth a professional look.

  • Brown or orange water on the first draw after the house has been quiet for hours.
  • Water pressure that is weak at several fixtures, not just one aerator.
  • Green or white crust, or small beads of moisture, on exposed copper or at fittings.
  • A second or third leak repair on the same system within a short stretch of time.
  • Visible rust blooms or flaking on old threaded steel pipe near the water heater or main shutoff.
  • Damp drywall or a musty smell inside a wall with no obvious source.

What you can safely check yourself

You do not need tools to gather the facts a contractor will ask for.

  1. Find your main shutoff and open the area around it

    Look at the pipe where water enters the house, usually in a basement or utility room. Note whether it is gray steel, copper-colored, or plastic.

  2. Run a magnet over exposed pipe

    A magnet sticks to steel and does not stick to copper or plastic. Gray, threaded pipe that attracts a magnet is galvanized steel.

  3. Draw water first thing in the morning

    Fill a clear glass from the tub tap. Rusty color that clears after a few seconds points to the pipes inside the house.

  4. Record every leak and repair

    Write dates and locations. This history is what separates a repair job from a repipe job.

  5. Photograph the pipe and the damage

    Photos help contractors quote more accurately and help you if you need to talk to your insurer.

A good repipe is mostly about what happens in the walls

The tubing is the easy part. The work is opening access, routing lines, pressure-testing before anything is closed up, and repairing drywall. Ask each contractor who patches walls, who paints, and whether the quote covers it or stops at the plumbing.

What drives the cost of a repipe

Cost follows the size of the house, the number of fixtures, whether walls are open or finished, and whether the home has a basement or crawl space for easy routing. Slab homes and plaster walls take more effort. Permit and inspection fees, drywall repair and paint are separate line items on many quotes, so ask for each in writing.

Coverage for water damage varies by policy, so ask your insurer, and photograph any leak damage first. Keep your repair history either way.

Older homes in the Columbia and Lancaster area

As general trade knowledge, homes built decades ago can have galvanized steel supply lines, and river boroughs founded in the 1700s have a mix of old and renovated houses. An older home in Columbia may have been partly updated, so one wall can have new copper while the next still has steel.

If you are comparing quotes for an older city home, Lancaster has its own permit paperwork, covered in the local note below. Never assume a neighbor's repipe tells you what yours needs. Only a look at your own pipes does.

Why PEX is not right for every job

PEX should not be left in direct sunlight, so it is a poor choice for exposed outdoor runs without protection. Some owners prefer metal for visible utility-room piping, and some water heaters need metal connectors near the unit. Your plumber should explain where each material goes and why.

If your main concern is cold weather, remember that PEX tolerates freezing better than rigid pipe but can still fail. Insulation and heat in unheated spaces matter just as much as the material.

Silver Spring homeowners: how to check a repipe contractor

Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires contractors doing $5,000 or more of home improvement in a year to register with the Office of Attorney General and show the registration number on contracts, estimates, proposals and ads. A written contract is required when the work costs more than $500 and a contractor may not demand payment before the contract is signed.

You can verify a registration at attorneygeneral.gov or by calling 1-888-520-6680. This applies to any plumber you hire, including us.

A homeowner can rescind a home improvement contract within three business days of signing, except as provided by law for emergencies. Permit rules vary, so ask the West Hempfield Township zoning and permits office or its building code inspector before work starts. The City of Lancaster's mechanical, electrical and plumbing permit application asks for a licensed plumber's name and license number and a certificate of insurance naming the City, so that is what to expect inside city limits.

Local facts here come from the Pennsylvania Attorney General home improvement brochure, the West Hempfield Township permit application and the City of Lancaster permit application.
