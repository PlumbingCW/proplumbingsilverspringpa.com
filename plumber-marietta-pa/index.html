Water in a Marietta basement: is it a pipe or the river?

Start with the water meter. Turn off every fixture and watch the dial for two minutes. If it keeps turning, a pipe is leaking, and that is a plumbing emergency we can handle at any hour, day or night.

If the dial is still and the Susquehanna is high or rain has been steady, the water is probably coming in from outside. Call 911 if the river is threatening your street, and stay out of a flooded basement with power on.

  • Close the main water valve if a pipe is the suspect.
  • Do not enter standing water near the panel, a water heater or a sump pump.
  • Photograph the water line on the wall with a timestamp if possible.
  • Call the 24/7 line for a pipe, drain or sewer problem.
  • Smell gas? Get everyone out, then call your gas utility or 911 away from the building.

What the Marietta river gauge tells a homeowner

The Susquehanna River at Marietta has a flood stage of 49 feet. The highest crest on record is 64.54 feet on June 23, 1972, from the remnants of Hurricane Agnes. Others include 60.73 feet in March 1936, 58.30 feet in June 1889, 58.16 feet in September 2011 from the remnants of Tropical Storm Lee, 56.80 feet in January 1996 from snowmelt plus rain, and 56.28 feet in September 2004 from the remnants of Hurricane Ivan.

The last flood at that gauge listed is July 26, 2018. For a homeowner, the lesson is that river-level water behaves differently from a plumbing leak. A sump pump moves groundwater but does not stop a river from rising, so check the gauge before a storm and move belongings early.

River-level flooding versus a plumbing failure

Both can leave water on the floor, but they call for different people. Use the table to decide who to call first.

Older homes in the historic districts

A house in the Marietta Historic District or the Chickies Historic District is likely to have been renovated more than once. Look for these signs of mixed piping.

  • A copper supply line spliced to rusting galvanized pipe at a basement wall.
  • Cast iron drains with newer plastic sections patched in.
  • Shutoff valves that were never replaced and do not close fully.
  • Rust-tinted water after a few hours unused.
  • A bathroom added later with a drain that gurgles when the kitchen runs.

Before We Arrive: readying a Marietta home

1

Close the main valve

Look where the line enters the foundation. Closing it takes a firm clockwise turn. In a flood, do not wade to a valve in rising water. Call 911 instead.

2

Shut off power in wet rooms

Switch off breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without crossing water. If the basement is flooded, stay out and tell the 24/7 dispatcher on the call.

3

Raise what you can

Lift boxes, rugs and appliances onto shelves or blocks. Move stored paint, cleaners and other chemicals up and away from the water, and keep them clear of the water heater.

4

Call the 24/7 emergency line

Say if the river is involved, whether the main is shut and which side of the borough you are on. Mention a historic-district address if you know the pipe is old.
