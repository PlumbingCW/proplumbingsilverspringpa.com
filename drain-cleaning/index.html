Emergency Drain Cleaning in Silver Spring, PA

Drain cleaning is the removal of a blockage from a sink, tub, shower or floor drain so water moves freely again. In Silver Spring, PA, our 24/7 line sends a plumber who clears the clog and checks that the pipe runs open.

Clogged drain right now: what to do while you wait

Our line answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and we book same-day appointments when available. A fixture that will not drain is rarely a safety problem by itself. Still, a full tub or a sink of dirty water can soak a floor quickly, so use the steps below while the plumber is on the way.

If a second fixture gurgles or rises too, the blockage may sit in the main line. Tell the dispatcher, and read about main sewer line cleaning so you know what that call involves.

  • Stop all water use, including the dishwasher and washing machine.
  • Bail standing water from the sink into a bucket instead of letting it sit.
  • Clear the cabinet under the sink and lay down a towel.
  • Put a towel at the base of a tub that is close to overflowing.
  • Leave the drain cleaner bottle on the shelf. Chemicals left in the pipe can burn the person who opens it later.
  • Note which fixtures are slow or backed up. That tells the plumber how deep the clog sits.

How snaking a drain works, step by step

Snaking, also called cabling, pushes a flexible steel cable with a cutting or hooked head through the pipe. It suits blockages close to the fixture, such as hair, a soap plug or a wad of paper.

  1. Choose the access point

    The plumber pulls the trap, opens a cleanout, or goes in through the fixture, whichever reaches the line with the least mess.

  2. Feed the cable

    A hand crank or small drum machine turns the cable as it advances. The plumber feels for the spot where it stops or springs back.

  3. Break up or hook the clog

    The turning head bores through soft material or snags it so it can be pulled out and bagged.

  4. Flush and test

    Hot water, then several fixtures run together, show whether the pipe is fully open or only partly clear.

  5. Reassemble and check joints

    The trap and fittings go back on, and the plumber watches for drips while water runs.

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, scraps and the disposal

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Cooking fat cools inside the pipe and coats the walls, then catches rice, coffee grounds and scraps until only a trickle gets through. The slowdown often builds for weeks before the sink stops.

If you have a food waste unit, run cold water and press the reset button on its underside. A disposal that hums but will not spin is a garbage disposal repair job rather than a drain job, and forcing it can burn out the motor.

Most kitchen branches yield to a hand cable or small drum machine. Packed grease sometimes needs a second pass after hot water softens it, so expect the plumber to run water between passes.

Shower and tub drains that empty slowly

Showers and tubs clog with hair and soap scum, usually within the first foot or two of the drain. Lifting the stopper or strainer and pulling out the wad clears many of them. If water still sits afterward, the trap below the tub may hold a soap plug that a short cable can reach.

A drain that leaks instead of clogs

A wet cabinet floor, a ceiling stain under a bathroom or damp drywall near a drain line can mean a leaking joint rather than a blockage. Drain leaks only show while water runs, so fill the fixture, release it and have someone watch below. Report where and when it drips, and the plumber can tell whether a fitting or a stretch of pipe needs replacing.

What you can try yourself, and where to stop

A few low-risk steps are worth trying before you call.

  • A cup plunger with a wet seal clears many sink clogs. Block the overflow opening with a damp rag first.
  • A small hand auger from a hardware store can reach a tub trap. Stop if the cable meets firm resistance.
  • Never mix chemical cleaners, and never plunge after pouring one in. Splashback burns skin and eyes.
  • Do not push a power auger through a fixture you cannot see into. It can scratch porcelain and kink the cable.
  • If a second fixture starts to gurgle or rise, stop and call. The problem is past the branch.

What drives the cost of drain cleaning

Cost follows where the blockage sits, how the pipe is reached and what the plumber finds. A clog in a trap a foot below a sink is a short job. A blockage beyond a second-floor bath, or one that needs a cleanout added to the line, takes longer.

Location of the clog

A blockage near the fixture is quick to reach. One beyond a bend or a floor away needs more cable and more time.

Access to the pipe

A removable trap or an existing cleanout speeds the job. A sealed line may need a fitting added first.

Type of blockage

Hair and soap clear fast. Packed grease or a foreign object can need repeat passes.

Number of drains involved

One fixture and a whole bathroom group are different scopes of work.

Time of the call

After-hours dispatch differs from a scheduled weekday visit, and the price is explained before work starts.

Drain Cleaning questions answered

Yes. The line is staffed 24/7, and the dispatcher asks which fixtures are affected, whether water is overflowing and whether it has reached a lower floor. Same-day visits are booked when the schedule allows. While you wait, stop running water and use the checklist near the top of this page.

Slow drainage usually means a partial blockage: a film of grease, soap and hair that narrows the pipe without sealing it. Water still passes, only more slowly. Left alone, it tends to end in a full clog at an inconvenient hour.

A plunger works on blockages within a few inches of the opening. A cable reaches the trap, the bend behind it and the branch beyond. Try the plunger first on a sink or tub, and call if one or two firm tries change nothing.

Careful cabling rarely harms sound plastic or copper. Old, scaled cast iron and cracked clay can suffer from aggressive tools, so the plumber who comes out picks the head and the force for the pipe in front of them. Tell them what the pipe is made of if you know.

Run water and watch the lowest drain in the house. If a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub backs up while you flush upstairs, the blockage is likely in the main line. Our page on clearing the main line covers that case.

A repeat clog usually means something is still in the pipe: scale on the wall, roots in a joint or a section that sags. Hydro jetting scours the pipe wall, and a camera look shows which of those is the cause.
