Boiler Repair in Silver Spring, PA

Boiler repair is the diagnosis and fix of a hot water or steam boiler that is not heating the house, holding pressure or staying dry.

Plumbers serve Silver Spring, PA, and nearby towns for no-heat calls, pressure faults, leaks and noisy pipes.

No heat: what to check before blaming the boiler

Start with the simple items. Confirm the thermostat is set to heat and above the room temperature, then check the boiler switch, the breaker and the fuel supply. A tripped breaker, an empty oil tank or a closed gas valve explains many cold mornings.

Look at the pressure gauge next. A reading near zero means the system has lost water, and a very high reading means pressure is building where it should not. Write down what you see, because the number tells the plumber where to begin.

Gas smell near the boiler: get outside first

A rotten-egg odor around the boiler or its pipes means you should walk out of the building straight away. Bring the household, leave the door open behind you, and skip the thermostat, the lights and any phone call until you are outdoors.

From outside, call your gas utility or 911 and wait for them to clear the house. Book the boiler visit only after the gas is made safe. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave and call 911 as well.

Hot water boilers versus steam boilers

A hot water boiler, also called hydronic, circulates heated water through baseboard or radiator loops with a pump. It stays full of water and relies on an expansion tank and a pressure gauge. A steam boiler heats water into steam that rises to radiators and falls back as condensate.

Steam systems depend on a correct water level, working traps and air vents, and a low water cutoff that protects the boiler. Never top off a steam boiler blindly, because the wrong water level can damage it. Tell the plumber which type you have and whether it burns gas or oil.

Boiler pressure: too high, too low or dropping

The pressure gauge is the quickest health check. Each maker lists a target range on the label or in the manual, so read that instead of trusting a number from the internet. A hot system reads higher than a cold one, but it should settle to a steady figure.

Low pressure usually means water is escaping or the auto-fill valve is not working. High pressure often points to a waterlogged expansion tank, and a relief valve that spits water is the boiler's way of warning you. Do not plug that valve, and do not keep refilling a system that keeps losing water.

Leaks at the boiler and in the heating loop

Common drip points include the relief valve, the circulator pump flange, radiator valves, threaded pipe joints and the bottom of the boiler. A drip from a valve or flange is a repair. Water that seeps from the boiler's casing or heat exchanger usually means the unit is nearing replacement.

If a leak is hidden inside a wall or floor, a burst pipe repair visit can open and fix the damaged section. Dry the area, mark the wet edge with tape and take photos so the plumber can see how fast it spreads.

What the noises mean

Boilers talk before they fail. These sounds are worth reporting.

  • Banging or knocking from the boiler, often caused by scale, trapped steam or a failing pump.
  • Gurgling in baseboards or radiators, which usually means air in the loop.
  • Whistling or kettle-like sounds, a hint of scale or restricted flow in the heat exchanger.
  • Clicking or ticking pipes as they expand, which is normal unless it is new and loud.
  • Hammering when a zone valve closes, a sign of water hammer in the pipes.
  • Rumbling at the burner, which can mean poor combustion and needs a technician.

Frozen pipes in cold weather: how to thaw safely

Our line runs 24/7, and winter no-heat calls often come with frozen pipes. Nearby Landisville averages about 23 inches of snow a year, so any pipe in an unheated garage, crawlspace or exterior wall can freeze.

  1. Open the nearest faucet

    A tap opened a little lets steam and meltwater escape as the ice clears.

  2. Look for a crack

    Check for bulges, frost lines or wet spots. If you see water, close the main valve and call.

  3. Warm the pipe gently

    Use a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels, working from the faucet side inward. Never use a torch or open flame.

  4. Protect the coldest rooms

    If the boiler is down, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and keep interior doors open so warmer air circulates.

  5. Call if you cannot reach it

    Pipes behind a wall or under a slab are a plumber's job. See our burst pipe service if one has split.

Safe checks versus work for a licensed plumber

You can safely check the thermostat, the breaker, a fuel gauge and the pressure reading, and you can bleed a radiator if you know how. Gas valves, burners, relief valves, combustion and venting are for a licensed plumber or heating technician.

Ask any company whether the technician who comes out works on your fuel and boiler type before booking, and ask us too. Fit a carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas and test it.

Older homes and radiators around Lancaster

Homes built decades ago can still run on radiators, cast iron piping and older boilers, and the area around Lancaster has a mix of old and renovated houses. That matters because aged parts tend to fail together, and a repair may lead to a conversation about replacing the boiler.

What affects boiler repair pricing

Boiler bills vary with the fault and the system. A bad circulator is a different job from a leaking heat exchanger. Clear price before work starts lets you compare a repair with a replacement.

System type

Steam, hot water, gas and oil boilers have different parts and test methods.

The failed component

A pump or valve is a smaller job than a heat exchanger or a control board.

Access

Tight utility rooms and old piping take longer to work around.

Age and condition

On an old boiler, the plumber weighs the repair against replacement.

Extra findings

Corroded valves, bad air vents or poor combustion may add work, and you hear about it first.

Coverage varies by policy, so ask your insurer, and photograph the gauge, the label and any water damage. Related work on hot water supply is on our water heater repair page.
